Cadence - Live Speech Console Help

Cadence is a real-time voice analysis console for interview practice. It runs entirely in-browser with no backend, ensuring no data leaves your machine.

Features:

  • WPM (Words Per Minute): Tracks your speaking pace
  • Volume (dB): Measures your speaking volume (displayed as -60 to 0 dB, where 0 is loudest and -60 is quietest)
  • Pitch (Hz): Analyzes your voice pitch
  • Filler Words: Detects "um", "uh", "like", "you know", etc.
  • Live Transcript: Real-time speech-to-text transcription
  • Post-Session Report: Generates charts and auto-generated feedback after each session

How to Use:

  1. Click "Start session" or press Spacebar
  2. Allow microphone access when prompted
  3. Speak naturally through your solution/explanation
  4. Click "Stop session" or press Spacebar when finished
  5. Review your session report with charts and feedback

Requirements:

  • Chrome or Edge browser required for speech recognition (WPM, transcript, filler detection)
  • Volume and pitch analysis work in all modern browsers
  • No installation required - runs entirely in-browser

Important Note:

Values shown are software-based estimates: The WPM, volume, pitch, and filler word measurements are software estimations based on audio analysis algorithms. They may not precisely match professional hardware measurements and should be used for relative improvement tracking rather than absolute scientific measurement.

Privacy:

All audio processing happens locally in your browser. No audio data is ever sent to a server or stored outside your machine.

Session data (metrics only) is stored locally in your browser's storage for history tracking.

Important Note About Measurements:

The measurements provided by Cadence (WPM, volume, pitch, etc.) are estimates based on software analysis of your audio input. These values are intended for relative comparison and self-improvement tracking, not as precise scientific measurements. Actual volume levels, pitch frequencies, and speaking rates may vary based on your microphone quality, distance from the mic, background noise, and other environmental factors. Use these metrics as general guides to help you become more aware of your speaking patterns rather than as precise diagnostic tools.

About Volume Scale:

The volume meter shows -60 to 0 dB, where 0 dB represents the loudest input (without distortion) and -60 dB represents very quiet sound. This follows standard audio measurement conventions where 0 dB is the maximum amplitude before clipping. In practice, normal speech typically ranges between -30 dB and -15 dB depending on speaking intensity and microphone distance.
